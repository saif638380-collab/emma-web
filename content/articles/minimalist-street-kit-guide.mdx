Carrying multiple heavy zoom lenses through urban transit is a guaranteed formula for fatigue and hesitation. The moment you spend ten seconds deciding whether to switch between 24mm, 50mm, or 85mm, the fleeting interaction on the sidewalk has already dissipated into the crowd.

Here is the exact kit I carried for 42 consecutive days of daily documentary shooting.

## 1. The Single Prime Lens Philosophy

When you restrict yourself to a single 35mm equivalent focal length, your feet become the focal length zoom. Your eyes develop an instinctive visual frame line before you even bring the finder to your eye. You know precisely where to stand on a subway platform or street corner to catch the geometry you want.

## 2. Handcrafted Leather Camera Strap

A heavy factory nylon strap screams tourist and rubs uncomfortably against your neck collar after four hours under the sun. 

I've tested dozens of options, but keep returning to the [Vegetable-Tanned Chestnut Leather Strap](affiliate:leather-strap). Over months of use, the oils from your hands soften the leather until it contours naturally around your wrist or shoulder, developing a rich dark patina.

## 3. Weather-Proof Modular Camera Sling

Bag design is notoriously prone to over-engineering. Most camera bags have too many zippers, velcro dividers that pull loose, and heavy padding that doubles their bulk.

The [Minimal Weather-Resistant 6L Sling](affiliate:camera-sling) strikes the balance. It carries one body with lens attached, an extra battery, lens wipe, and notebook, resting tight against the lower back so it doesn't swing when you lean forward over bridges or stairways.

## 4. Optical Discipline

- Never wipe coated optics with shirt hems; always carry clean microfibre cloths
- Leave the lens hood on at all times—it protects against bumps far better than cheap UV filters that introduce flare
- Set your camera to mechanical shutter to prevent banding under LED subway lights

Simplicity in gear is not asceticism; it is freedom from the friction of equipment.
