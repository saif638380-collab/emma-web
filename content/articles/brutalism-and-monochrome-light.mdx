Raw concrete has an austere honesty. Unlike polished glass curtain walls that reflect whatever chaos passes in front of them, board-formed concrete records the grain of the wooden molds that held it while curing. It absorbs the harsh midday sun and turns harsh light into soft mathematical gradients.

## Stripping Down the Color Noise

When photographing brutalist structures, color often acts as an unnecessary distraction. Blue skies and green foliage compete with the tonal range of gray cast aggregate.

By shifting the internal profile to a high-contrast monochrome mode with a deep red filter simulation, the sky drops into an ink-like charcoal, and the sunlit concrete ridges jump forward into sculptured relief.

> "Architecture is the learned game, correct and magnificent, of forms assembled in the light." — Le Corbusier

## Key Architectural Techniques

When shooting large public structures:
1. **Wait for oblique raking light**: The 45-degree angle in late afternoon highlights subtle texture in poured concrete.
2. **Align the vertical axis**: Any slight keystone tilt distorts the weight of the cantilever. Keep your sensor plane parallel to the building wall.
3. **Include negative space**: Let the sky occupy at least 40% of the composition to give the heavy concrete room to breathe.
